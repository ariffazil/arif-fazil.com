Geological framework & primary observations

Observed architecture

The Dangerous Grounds represent thinned continental crust (15–20 km Moho) rifted during Paleogene South China Sea opening. The Sabah Trough forms a prominent bathymetric deep (~2.9 km water depth). Inboard lies the Middle Miocene to Holocene fold-thrust belt, containing the seismically mapped Pagasa/North Sabah mobile shale wedge. Mount Kinabalu is a Late Miocene granitic pluton (U-Pb ~7.85–7.22 Ma; older K-Ar ages contested) exhumed rapidly to 4,095 m elevation.

Competing working models

Four distinct mechanisms are evaluated without dogma: Model C posits foredeep flexural bending and continental underthrusting; Model B highlights gravity-driven mobile shale tectonics; Model A evaluates sparse sill-dyke thermal plumbing; and Model D integrates deep lithospheric slab remnants observed in teleseismic tomography.

Extensional vs null hypothesis

Fault-growth thickening observed on regional 3D seismic dip lines suggests a younger Neogene extensional pulse contributing to trough accommodation. However, the rigorous baseline null hypothesis is foredeep flexural bending: downward flexure of the lower plate naturally generates outer-trench normal faulting without requiring regional plate-boundary extension.

1. Dangerous Grounds: Inherited thinned continental margin

Attenuated continental lithosphere rifted during Paleogene South China Sea opening, draped by syn-rift and post-rift strata. High-velocity buildups along the trough NW flank are non-unique on seismic — evaluate at least three rival hypotheses: (H1) Oligo-Miocene carbonate platform (falsifier: low PEF/high clay-OH or volcaniclastic cuttings); (H2) altered volcaniclastic/tuffaceous lookalike (falsifier: clean limestone wireline/core matrix); (H3) local basement horst/igneous intrusion (falsifier: stratified internal seismic reflectors and normal burial thermal maturity).

Observational status
Constrained by regional 2D/3D reflection seismic and ocean drilling (Hinz & Schlüter 1985; Hutchison 2005).
Geographic scope
Dangerous Grounds western margin segment (0–242 km geodesic chainage).
Geological timing
Paleogene rifting (~45–32 Ma) terminating prior to or coeval with seafloor spreading cessation (~16–15 Ma).
Key literature
Hinz, K. and Schlüter, H.U. (1985), Energy 10: 297–315.

2. Sabah Trough: Accommodation mechanisms & bathymetric deep

The Sabah Trough is an enigmatic ~2.9–3.0 km deep bathymetric depression (GEBCO_2019 trough floor −3,015.7 m at km 241.7). Primary models for accommodation include: (1) Foredeep flexural down-bending under the load of the adjacent Crocker Range and Northwest Borneo fold-thrust wedge; (2) Residual bathymetric low inherited from oceanic-continental transition; and (3) Neogene extensional reactivation creating tectonic space.

Observational status
Water depth measured by multibeam bathymetry and seismic reflection; structural accommodation mechanism is interpretive.
Geographic scope
Sabah Trough axial depression (242–290 km geodesic chainage).
Geodynamic alternatives
Flexural bending predicts asymmetric deepening toward the fold-thrust front. In contrast, extensional reactivation predicts symmetric syn-growth wedges.

3. Fold-Thrust Belt: Shortening, mobile shale canopy & gravity spreading

The offshore Northwest Borneo toe-thrust system is dominated by gravity-driven detached fold-and-thrust deformation above an overpressured Middle Miocene shale detachment (Pagasa/Setap Formation), with canopy coalescence tied to UIU (~10.5 Ma, <0.1 Myr burst) and capped by SRU (~8.5–9 Ma per Morley 2023; CONTRADICTION: ~9.4 Ma from 3 wells in NN10 — unresolved). Seismic studies document shale diapirs, mud volcanoes, and mini-basins, demonstrating that shallow shortening is driven by sedimentary overburden and slope gradient rather than pure crustal plate convergence.

Northern-vs-Central Sabah Scope Law (Morley et al. 2023): The North Sabah–Pagasa Mobile Shale Wedge (NSPW) is confined to northern Sabah and dies out southward near ~6.0°N. Because T-KIN-XS-833 reaches the coast at Kota Kinabalu (5.98°N), Model B represents an along-strike northern-Sabah structural end-member projected onto the regional margin context — not an assumption that the NSPW is uniform across all of NW Sabah.

Observational status
Imaged in high-resolution 3D seismic reflection surveys (Morley et al. 2023; King et al. 2010; Wu, McClay & de Vera 2020 envelope).
Geographic scope
Outboard fold-thrust belt (290–372.4 km geodesic chainage; toe-thrust belt 324.8 km, shelf edge 372.4 km).
Key literature
Morley, C.K. et al. (2023), Geosphere 19(1): 291; Wu, J.E., McClay, K. & de Vera, J. (2020), Geosphere 16(1): 329–356; King, R.C. et al. (2010), Mar. Pet. Geol. 27: 238–246.

4. Extensional Reactivation Hypothesis: Seismic fault growth vs flexural bending

Author-reported 3D seismic observations on select dip lines reveal normal faults with apparent syn-growth stratigraphic expansion, motivating an OPEN / WORKING HYPOTHESIS (CL-XS-YOUNGER-EXT) for a younger post-spreading extensional pulse. Crucially, normal faulting in this structural position must be weighed against the baseline null hypothesis — Model C (foredeep flexural bending) — because downward flexure of a lower plate naturally generates outer-trench-slope normal faults without requiring regional plate-scale extension. Guard: software-default NORMAL is not proof of extension; fault-trace geometry or attribute counts alone cannot settle regional kinematics.

Observational status
OPEN / WORKING HYPOTHESIS; requires quantitative syn-growth expansion ratios, independent biostratigraphy (nannofossil NN-zone ranges, distinguishing cuttings LADs from SWC/core), calibrated time–depth conversion, and balanced strain restoration.
Geographic scope
Sabah Trough flank and outer toe-thrust margin.
Critical test
Detailed biostratigraphic dating (NN-zone ranges, not single points) and horizon-thickness restoration of syn-growth vs post-growth strata across the diachronous, composite Deep Regional Unconformity (DRU 11.7–13.77 Ma; CONTRADICTION: GTS2012 gives 14.2/14.91 Ma — unresolved).

5. Mount Kinabalu: Pluton emplacement, rapid exhumation & regional setting

Mount Kinabalu (4,095 m) is Southeast Asia's highest peak, composed of a Late Miocene granitic pluton (U-Pb zircon ~7.85–7.22 Ma; older K-Ar ages contested). Thermochronology demonstrates extremely rapid exhumation (~7 mm/yr between 7.8 and 7.2 Ma). While onshore extension is cited as accommodating pluton ascent, no direct magmatic or structural conduit links the offshore Sabah Trough to the pluton.

Observational status
U-Pb zircon crystallisation 7.85±0.08–7.22±0.07 Ma (Cottam et al. 2010); (U-Th)/He cooling thermochronology (Cottam et al. 2013). Older K-Ar ages (~14 Ma) remain contested — the intrusion age is PLAUSIBLE, not settled.
Geographic scope
Onshore Crocker Range hinterland (chainage 484.9 km on transect projection).
Key literature
Cottam, M.A. et al. (2010), J. Geol. Soc. 167: 49–60; Cottam, M.A. et al. (2013), J. Geol. Soc. 170: 805–816.

Deep lithospheric & mantle framework — Model D

Teleseismic receiver-function and tomography studies provide critical mantle depth constraints, kept distinct from shallow crustal transect interpolations.

PILIA ET AL. 2023

Eastern Sabah mantle downwelling

Teleseismic tomography in eastern Sabah records a high-velocity mantle downwelling beneath the Dent/Semporna peninsula, interpreted as subducted oceanic lithosphere or lithospheric dripping (Nature Geoscience, doi:10.1038/s41561-023-01201-7).

CORNWELL ET AL. 2025 · MODEL D

45–55 km teleseismic discontinuity

P-wave receiver functions identify a 75–100 km wide dipping seismic discontinuity at 45–55 km depth beneath central Sabah, interpreted as a remnant oceanic slab tied to Celebes Sea subduction (JGR Solid Earth, doi:10.1029/2025JB031466).

SIDEK ET AL. 2016 · GILLIGAN ET AL. 2026

Moho depth anchors

On-line receiver-function anchors measure 32.8 ± 2.5 km at KMY (Kota Kinabalu coast, 429.9 km, 0.0 km off-line; Sidek et al. 2016) — falsifying localized >42 km lithospheric drip/root predictions beneath the KK/Crocker axis — and 30–35 km at KINA (Mt. Kinabalu, 486.7 km, 0.9 km off-line; Gilligan et al. 2026). In central Sabah, SBC5 (566.6 km, 25 km) and SBC6 (584.3 km, 24 km) are explicitly marked CONTESTED side-by-side with Cornwell et al. (2025)'s reading (mid-crustal discontinuity above a 34–38 km WSW-dipping LVZ). Unconstrained intervals (0–425 km offshore; 600–832.8 km east, stations >50 km off-line) carry no interpolated Moho bridge. Footnote: MetMalaysia station MTM (~60 km) lacks public FDSN coordinates and cannot be georeferenced onto T-KIN-XS-833 (doi:10.1029/2025JB031499).

Tectonic evolution & chronological storyboard

Five sequential evolutionary stages illustrate the geodynamic history of the Northwest Borneo margin from continental breakup to the present day.

Fault-growth geometry diagnostic (CL-XS-YOUNGER-EXT · OPEN / WORKING HYPOTHESIS)

Fault-trace geometry or fault-type counts alone cannot prove regional plate-scale extension (baseline null hypothesis: Model C, foredeep flexural bending). Resolving CL-XS-YOUNGER-EXT requires four independent discriminators: (1) quantitative syn-growth expansion ratios across dated regional horizons, (2) independent biostratigraphic age control distinguishing cuttings LADs from SWC/core, (3) calibrated time–depth conversion, and (4) balanced structural restoration testing whether updip shelf extension + flexural bending account for the strain budget.

Stage 1: Paleogene continental rifting & breakup (Dangerous Grounds)

Eocene to Early Miocene extensional thinning of the South China margin created rotated continental fault blocks and rift basins, subsequently draped by post-rift marine sediments and carbonate platforms (Hinz & Schlüter 1985).

Stage 2: Early–Middle Miocene collision, margin loading & foredeep flexure

Collision of the Dangerous Grounds with the Crocker accretionary wedge (BMU / TCU, ~23–24 Ma; Lunt 2022 JAES v.230) separated Paleogene Crocker/syn-rift strata from Neogene wedge accommodation and terminated proto-South China Sea subduction. Subsequent Middle Miocene margin loading developed the diachronous, composite Deep Regional Unconformity (DRU 11.7–13.77 Ma (CONTRADICTION: GTS2012 14.2/14.91 Ma — unresolved); nannofossil NN6–NN7 range, not a single collision datum; Lunt 2022 BGSM 74; Morley et al. 2023 — "mixtures of conformable regions together with local unconformities") at the intra-Middle Miocene Stage III/IV boundary, ~3.5–4 Myr younger than Sarawak MMU (~16 Ma). In northern Sabah, mobile-shale canopy coalescence peaked at UIU (~10.5 Ma, <0.1 Myr burst) and was capped by SRU (~8.5–9 Ma per Morley 2023; CONTRADICTION: ~9.4 Ma, 3 NN10 wells) (Morley et al. 2023).

Stage 3: Proposed younger Neogene extensional reactivation (OPEN / WORKING HYPOTHESIS)

Seismic dip profiles in select 3D volumes display apparent syn-growth strata thickening into normal faults cutting above the DRU, motivating an open working hypothesis (CL-XS-YOUNGER-EXT) for episodic younger extension. This remains untested against the baseline null hypothesis (Model C, foredeep flexural bending) pending calibrated expansion ratios, biostratigraphy, time–depth ties, and balanced restoration.

Stage 4: Late Miocene magmatism & Kinabalu pluton emplacement (7–8 Ma)

Ascent and rapid cooling of the Mount Kinabalu granitic pluton at U-Pb ~7.85–7.22 Ma (older K-Ar ages contested), accommodated by localized crustal dilation and followed by rapid exhumation. Model A explicitly separates (1) older/inherited Miocene volcaniclastics or localized mafic components on the rifted trough flank (permissible without requiring a present-day thermal anomaly) from (2) untested Late Miocene (7–8 Ma) Kinabalu-linked offshore sills or dykes (off by default; no continuous magmatic conduit is imaged across the Crocker Range).

Stage 5: Pliocene to present-day gravitational overprint & seismicity

Continued deltaic progradation drives gravity-driven shale tectonics and toe-thrusting in the outboard fold belt. Modern seismicity (e.g. the 2015 Mw 6.0 Ranau earthquake) demonstrates ongoing extensional faulting and uplift onshore.

Mount Kinabalu: Geodynamic relationship to offshore tectonics

Synthesizing onshore geochronological and petrological constraints with the offshore structural history.

Geochronology

Zircon U-Pb crystallization ages cluster tightly at 7–8 Ma (Late Miocene; Cottam et al. 2010). The offshore younger-extension proposal lacks absolute age calibration, leaving direct coeval timing unconfirmed.

Tectonic setting

Onshore structural studies document local NW–SE extension during granite ascent. However, whether this reflects a regional lithospheric extensional regime or localized tectonic escape remains an active question.

Plumbing architecture

Regional mapping reveals no continuous magmatic dyke swarm or sill complex linking the deepwater Sabah Trough to the onshore Kinabalu pluton across the intervening Crocker Range.

Uplift vs exhumation

Thermochronology demonstrates rapid exhumation (~7 mm/yr) around 7 Ma, but the modern peak elevation (4,095 m) reflects younger Plio-Pleistocene surface uplift driven by mantle buoyancy, delamination, or isostatic rebound.

Testing criteria & falsification thresholds

What physical measurements would decisively discriminate between models?

  • Calibrated Biostratigraphic Ties: Drilling and dating the syn-growth wedges across the Deep Regional Unconformity (DRU). If fault growth is confined to the pre-Middle Miocene, the younger-extension hypothesis is falsified in favor of inherited rifting and foredeep flexure.
  • Deep Crustal OBS Seismology: Deploying ocean-bottom seismometers across the unconstrained 0–425 km offshore gap. Imaging continuous Dangerous Grounds continental crust beneath the fold-thrust belt would confirm Model C (underthrusting), whereas finding oceanic crust would support a remnant suture.
  • Thermal & Geochemical Sampling: Core sampling of candidate offshore saucer-shaped bodies. Distinguishing true igneous sills from overpressured mud pipes via petrography and organic geochemistry would definitively resolve Model A.
  • Kinematic Strain Balancing: Restoring regional 3D seismic lines from the shelf edge to the abyssal plain. If toe-thrust shortening equals updip shelf normal faulting, gravitational spreading (Model B) fully accounts for the strain budget.

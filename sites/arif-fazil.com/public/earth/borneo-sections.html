03 — DIP SECTIONB–B′ · hinterland to rifted fragments

487 km from the Rajang–Crocker hinterland, across a wide shallow shelf carrying up to ten kilometres of Neogene deltaics, through the deepwater fold–thrust belt, into the NW Borneo Trough and out onto the thinned continental blocks of the Dangerous Grounds.

Sea floor and topography along B–B′. measured Sampled from the global DEM at 701 points along a great-circle transect. Land rises to 1753 m in the hinterland; the deepest point is 2542 m at 319 km along section. Note the extraordinarily flat shelf between 129 and 205 km — water depth stays between 3 and 190 m across 76 km.
Geological section along B–B′. Sea floor is measured real. Sediment thickness, the basal detachment, growth faults and thrusts are interpreted/schematic from published work. Vertical exaggeration ≈ 13×; at this exaggeration a 3° dip looks like 35°. Read the numbers, not the steepness.
Free-air gravity along B–B′. measured Sandwell & Smith V34.1, 1 arc-minute. This is a compatibility test, not an independent confirmation: gravity rises to +94 mGal over the Borneo hinterland, falls to -37 mGal across the trough and fold belt, then recovers to about +20 mGal over the Dangerous Grounds blocks. A 131 mGal swing is compatible with a transition from thick continental crust to thinned crust and back — but the free-air field is non-unique: it also responds to bathymetry, sediment load and basement relief. Bouguer correction plus a forward model (water, sediment, upper/lower crust, mantle, Moho-depth and density sensitivity) is required before any crustal thickness may be claimed from it (ledger CL-BO-GRAV-SWING-131). Onshore values are from the land compilation and carry higher uncertainty than the marine altimetry — treat the far-left peak as indicative, not precise.
Crustal architecture along B–B′. Moho depth from published gravity inversions and refraction (interpreted). Crystalline crust thins from about 33 km beneath the hinterland to roughly 13 km under the trough, then thickens slightly to 15–16 km beneath the Dangerous Grounds blocks. Moho deepens from about 21 km under the trough to 35 km under Borneo. Vertical exaggeration ≈ 2.6×, an honest scale for a crustal section.

Reading the section, segment by segment

  1. 0–100 km · accreted basement. Cretaceous–Eocene deepwater clastics of the Rajang and Crocker groups, deformed and locally metamorphosed. This is the source terrain, not a reservoir target. Relief reaches 1753 m and gravity peaks at +94 mGal — the highest value on the transect.
  2. 100–129 km · coastal plain. Baram and Belait deltaics onlap the accretionary complex. Sediment thickens from under a kilometre to roughly five.
  3. 129 km · coastline at 4.7149°N 114.5191°E. From here the section is entirely marine.
  4. 129–205 km · the shelf. Water depth stays between 3 and 190 m across 76 km — one of the flattest shelves anywhere. Beneath it, the late Neogene deltaic wedge reaches ten kilometres. This load is the engine: it drives the gravity tectonics downdip and it overpressures the section. Gravity settles to about +25 mGal.
  5. 205–230 km · shelf break and upper slope. Listric growth faults detach onto a deep shale horizon. Sea floor steepens from under 0.1° to 2–3°. Updip extension here balances downdip shortening — the classic paired system.
  6. 230–330 km · deepwater fold–thrust belt. Water 1,500–2,540 m. Six to eight thrust-cored anticlines at 12–15 km spacing; thrusts dip landward at 15–40° and sole into a detachment 6–9 km below the sea floor. Sea-floor expression on the youngest folds shows interpreted geologically young deformation — it does not by itself prove the belt is slipping today; "active now" would require modern seismicity, geodesy, growth strata reaching the seabed, or repeat bathymetry (ledger CL-BO-YOUNG-MORPH). Gravity drops to a mean of −13 mGal.
  7. 330–365 km · NW Borneo Trough. Deepest point 2542 m at 319 km. This is interpreted as a fossil subduction trench — the widely used regional model says convergence stopped in the Middle Miocene when thinned continental crust of the Dangerous Grounds entered the zone and choked it; the exact geometry, timing and continuity of Proto-South China Sea consumption remain model-dependent and debated (ledger CL-BO-FOSSIL-TRENCH). On this reading the trough is a scar, not a modern plate boundary.
  8. 365–440 km · Dangerous Grounds blocks. Sea floor rises to about −1,100 m and gravity recovers to roughly +22 mGal. Crystalline crust 13–16 km in fault-bounded blocks separated by more-thinned corridors.
  9. 440–487 km · Dangerous Grounds basin. Sea floor deepens again to about −2,280 m beneath roughly five kilometres of post-rift fill over thinned continental crust. This is the same crustal family that carries the North Luconia carbonate build-ups — but 400 km further basinward, and not an analogue you can transfer without saying so.

04 — STRIKE SECTIONA–A′ · along the fold belt

295 km inside the deepwater belt, parallel to the folds. A strike section is the wrong tool for imaging dip-direction structure — and it is included precisely because it shows what a dip line cannot: along-strike continuity, culminations, saddles and lateral change.

Geological section along strike, A–A′. Entirely deepwater, 1640–2440 m. Sea floor is measured. Two detachment levels are shown because the belt is documented as a multiple-detachment system. Vertical exaggeration ≈ 9×.
Free-air gravity along strike, A–A′. measured The along-strike mean shifts from −9 mGal over the first 100 km to −21 mGal over the last 45 km: the belt sits deeper, structurally, toward the north-east. That is a real gradient in crustal thickness, not noise — and it is the kind of thing a single dip line cannot show you.
The honest limitation of a strike section. Thrusts here strike N51°E; this section runs at N50°E. The faults are therefore sub-parallel to the line of section, so they image as steep, discontinuous traces rather than true cross-sections. Do not try to restore or balance this panel. It is here to demonstrate continuity and lateral variation, and to make the point that the belt varies along strike more than a single dip line suggests.

The argument that matters, and it is unresolved. Whether the deepwater fold–thrust belt is driven by gravity (delta loading) or by basement-involved crustal shortening has been argued for thirty years. The current position is the least satisfying one and the most likely correct: both, in different places at the same time. Offshore Brunei and southern Sabah the belt is gravity-driven, with downdip shortening balancing updip extension. Offshore northern Sabah, shortening exceeds extension, which requires a basement contribution. The two systems interfere over a zone about 15 km wide. Any prospect evaluation that assumes a single mechanism across the whole belt is wrong somewhere.

06 — STRATIGRAPHYThe Sarawak Cycles

⚠ Correlation firewall — these cycles are calibrated in SARAWAK, not Sabah

No formal crosswalk yet exists between the Sarawak Cycles I–VIII, Sabah's Stage IVA–IVE terminology, and the Kinabalu SB4–SB8 sequence-boundary scheme — internal project records show those correlations are still being resolved from biostratigraphy and key-line seismic. Until the KL2 databank (80+ wells) publishes a confidence-bearing crosswalk, do not read cycle numbers onto SB horizons or onto Kinabalu Basin sequences (ledger: CL-SW-CYCLES-CROSSWALK).

The Sarawak Basin is divided into eight transgressive–regressive packages bounded by regional unconformities. For deepwater work, three of them carry the argument:

Cycle VIII is the load. Pliocene to Recent deltaics, delivered fast enough to overpressure the section and drive the fold belt. Without this, nothing downdip deforms.

Cycle VII is the prize. Late Miocene to Pliocene turbidites, folded into the hanging-wall anticlines that carry Kikeh, Gumusut and Kebabangan.

Cycle V is the analogue trap. Central Luconia build-ups — the carbonate province that everyone in the deepwater thinks about and that does not transfer to North Luconia simply because both are called Luconia.

And the thing that decides column height. Seal. Across this entire margin the seal is the least quantified element in the published record: no public capillary entry pressure measurements, no mercury injection dataset, no leak-off tests in the public domain. Build-ups are bought on amplitude and sold on seal, and the seal is the part nobody has measured.

07 — LIMITSWhat would change this, and what cannot be claimed

Stated plainly, because a section that does not say where it is weak invites someone to build a decision on it.

LimitationConsequenceWhat would fix it
No proprietary seismicFault and fold geometry is representative, not picked. Depths to individual structures are not predictive.A depth-migrated 2D line, or a 3D volume, across the same transect.
Global DEM vertical accuracy ±100–200 m in deep water Sea-floor relief smaller than that is not real signal. Multibeam bathymetry, or GEBCO 15 arc-second subset.
Onshore free-air gravity from land compilation The +94 mGal hinterland peak is indicative, not precise. Terrestrial gravity survey, or an EGM2008/EGM2020 evaluation.
Crustal thickness at 1° effective resolution Moho geometry is a smooth regional surface. It cannot show a local crustal block. Wide-angle OBS refraction along the line.
Sediment thickness from literature, not from this dataset The wedge is drawn to published values, so it cannot be independently checked here. Public-domain sediment thickness grid (Divins/NOAA), or seismic.
Two lines onlyNo 3D control. Along-strike variation between A–A′ and the rest of the belt is unconstrained.A grid of parallel dip lines, or one 3D survey.
What this plate will not do. It will not give you a volumetric estimate, a chance of success, or a prospect ranking. It is a regional framework — the thing you show someone to explain where they are, not the thing you show a partner committee to justify a well. Confusing those two is how a well gets drilled on a regional map.

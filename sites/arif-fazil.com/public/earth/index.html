For the extra curious · how we know these ages

The Hadean–Archean boundary (~4,000 Ma) is marked by the oldest known rocks on Earth: the Acasta Gneiss in Canada's Slave Craton (4.03 Ga). The Archean–Proterozoic boundary (~2,500 Ma) coincides with the Great Oxidation Event — free oxygen first appearing in the atmosphere, a signature of cyanobacterial photosynthesis. The Proterozoic–Phanerozoic boundary (539 Ma) is the Cambrian Explosion: within ~20 million years, almost every animal body plan we know appears in the fossil record. We are 539 million years into the Phanerozoic and about 300,000 years into Homo sapiens. The bar is not fair — and that is the lesson.

CASE STUDY · THE FOUNDER'S HOME WATERS

◈ The Malay Basin — a tape recorder that became a gas province

The globe opens looking at the Malay Basin for a reason. This is where the founder spent 13+ years reading rocks offshore Peninsular Malaysia. What follows is the basin's story — from tectonic birth to petroleum system to the wells that proved it.

How a sag becomes a province

In the Oligo–Miocene, the Sundaland crust stretched in a back-arc setting, faults dropped the basement into half-grabens, and ~30 million years of sediment poured in — lacustrine source rocks at the base, fluvio-deltaic reservoirs above, all sealed by marine mudstones as the sea returned.

Heat did the rest. The tape-recorder became a petroleum system: one of Southeast Asia's great gas provinces. Reading that record — seismic, well logs, geochemistry — is what an exploration geoscientist does, and it is the human craft the GEOX agentic engine: evidence-gated, human-decided.

In 2025, the LEBAH EMAS-1 wildcat opened a new play fairway along the Western Hinge Fault — proof that even a mature basin can surprise you. The four founder wells below are the ones the sovereign actually signed off on. Their coordinates are withheld; their existence is witness enough.

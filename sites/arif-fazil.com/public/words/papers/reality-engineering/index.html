Reality Engineering for Governed Agent Systems

A Constitutional Runtime Architecture and Falsifiable Research Agenda

V0.9 DRAFT 11 SECTIONS 2 APPENDICES 16 REFERENCES POSITION PAPER
Abstract. Agent systems can improve their prompts, tools, memory, and control code while remaining unable to determine whether their claims correspond to the external world or whether they are authorized to act. This position paper defines Reality Engineering as the design of interfaces that force machine claims and actions into auditable contact with evidence, authority, and observed consequences. It presents arifOS as a running case: a classical, model-agnostic constitutional runtime that places non-compensatory policy gates around agent execution. The architecture separates proposer, judge, executor, and witness roles; assigns every task an evidence floor; records predictions before outcomes; and retains falsifications rather than overwriting them. The paper does not claim artificial general intelligence (AGI), artificial superintelligence (ASI), quantum computation, or a physical law. Its defensible classification is an agentic governance operating layer: potentially useful for governing future AGI/ASI systems, but neither the intelligence substrate nor evidence that such systems have been achieved. Four falsifiable hypotheses and a preregisterable evaluation protocol are specified. A 19 October 2026 internal audit snapshot is disclosed as engineering telemetry, not efficacy evidence: 131 prediction records, 44 marked verified, 2 marked unverifiable, and 85 lacking either status in the exported aggregate. The central proposition is that increasingly self-modifying agents require a correctness and authority layer that they cannot silently rewrite.

Keywords: AI agents · governance kernel · agent operating system · evidence provenance · capability security · runtime assurance · human oversight · falsifiability

1Introduction

The performance of an agent depends on more than model weights. A harness determines which context is shown, which tools are available, how failures are retried, what is remembered, and how outputs are checked. Recent systems optimize this surrounding code automatically. Self-Harness reports gains from a fixed model revising a bounded harness through weakness mining, proposal, and regression validation [1]. Meta-Harness treats harness code as a search object and gives a proposer access to prior source, scores, and execution traces [2]. Matched-budget evaluation, however, shows that harness evolution can fail to outperform simpler test-time scaling and may generalize weakly to held-out tasks [3]. These results expose a control problem: when the machinery that produces behavior can rewrite itself, what remains outside the optimization loop and who is authorized to change it?

This paper proposes a narrow answer: engineer explicit contact surfaces between agent claims and independently inspectable evidence, between proposed actions and authority, and between predictions and later outcomes. The term Reality Engineering has prior use in advertising research, where it described the blurring of commercial signification and popular culture [4]. No priority over the phrase is claimed. The present contribution is a distinct operational definition for agent infrastructure.

The paper makes three contributions. First, it gives a typed architecture for claims, evidence, authority, execution, outcomes, and falsification. Second, it resolves several apparent contradictions in the arifOS design by separating scientific, architectural, and metaphorical statements. Third, it converts the strongest proposed benefits into a falsifiable evaluation protocol rather than reporting unperformed experiments as results.

Scope. arifOS is analyzed as a running design and prototype, not as proof of a universal theory. The work is a position and systems-architecture paper. It reports a descriptive store snapshot but no controlled efficacy result, no independent replication, and no quantum computation.

2Operational Definition

Definition 1 — Reality Engineering Reality Engineering is the design and maintenance of mechanisms by which an agent's claims and proposed actions are (i) bounded by their available evidence, (ii) checked against delegated authority before external effects, and (iii) corrected when witnessed outcomes contradict prior predictions.

The definition distinguishes six objects:

  1. Experience: what an actor or observer underwent.
  2. Evidence: a trace, measurement, document, or observation with provenance.
  3. Interpretation: meaning assigned to evidence.
  4. Model: a compressed account used to explain or predict.
  5. Prediction: a time-stamped claim about an outcome not yet observed.
  6. Authority: a revocable permission to mutate a specified resource under stated constraints.

Reality is not equated with the evidence store. Evidence is a fallible access channel. Consequently, "reality overrides the model" is implemented as a correction rule: sufficiently qualified counterevidence changes the system's warranted claim status; it is not a claim of direct, infallible access to reality.

4Reality-Contact Architecture

Figure 1 shows the architecture as a sequence of typed gates. The language model is a proposer. It does not acquire authority from eloquence, confidence, benchmark score, access to a tool schema, or possession of evidence.

4.1 Four separated powers

The architecture separates four functions:

  • Proposer (Reflex): generates candidate claims or actions.
  • Judge: evaluates policy, evidence class, reversibility, and required authorization.
  • Executor: performs an allowed effect through constrained tools.
  • Witness: records what was requested, authorized, executed, and observed.

No single component should propose, authorize, execute, and certify the same consequential action. Separation does not guarantee truth: components may share a model family, data, or operator and therefore fail correlatively. It creates inspectable boundaries and permits stronger independence where risk warrants it.

4.2 Non-compensatory gates

A weighted score can conceal a catastrophic failure: excellent usefulness can numerically offset absent authority. arifOS therefore applies hard vetoes before any soft aggregation. Let a proposed transition be τ, hard predicates be hi(τ) ∈ {0, 1}, and soft dimensions be zj(τ) ∈ (0, 1]. The transition score is defined only when every hard predicate passes:

The geometric mean is retained as a design choice for soft dimensions because it penalizes low components and is scale-symmetric under the stated normalization. No uniqueness theorem is claimed here: such a theorem requires explicit axioms and proof. The previously used "Grand Equation" is therefore demoted from scientific law to configurable governance functional.

4.3 Verdict semantics

The runtime uses a restrictive lattice rather than a scalar approval probability:

Void ≺ 888-Hold ≺ Hold ≺ Sabar ≺ Seal

Void blocks a prohibited transition. 888-Hold requires explicit human confirmation for irreversible or high-impact action. Hold pauses for missing evidence or authority. Sabar permits continued bounded investigation without external commitment. Seal authorizes only the scoped transition recorded in the receipt. A verdict is not a claim that the output is true; it states the transition's governance status.

4.4 Evidence layers

Each task declares a required evidence floor and a current evidence layer. Figure 2 prevents inferred text from silently becoming verified state.

4.5 Reality Graph and temporal loop

The Reality Graph is a typed, versioned graph over identity, relationship, authority, consequence, and observed-world records. Claims may have falsified-by and awaiting-witness edges. Falsified claims remain addressable; deletion would erase learning and make calibration unverifiable. Authority edges are checked before mutation, not appended afterward as decorative audit metadata.

A temporal service freezes a prediction before its outcome window, then binds later observations to that prediction. The minimal record is

(2) R = ⟨ id, tp, C, Ep, W, to, O, Q, V ⟩

where tp is prediction time, C the claim, Ep evidence available at prediction time, W the outcome window, to observation time, O the observation, Q witness qualifications, and V the adjudicated result. Changes create successor records; they do not rewrite R.

8Falsifiable Research Programme

Four hypotheses translate the architecture into experiments.

H1: Silent-drift reduction. On matched long-horizon tasks with injected state changes, a stack with frozen predictions, outcome binding, and retained falsifications will have a lower rate of false states that persist unchallenged than a score-only agent stack.

H2: Recovery latency. After injection of a false belief into memory, a provenance-typed stack will require fewer action steps and less wall-clock time to restore the correct operational state than an untyped conversational-memory baseline.

H3: Authority containment. Under adversarial tool descriptions and prompt injection, a pre-commit capability gate will produce fewer unauthorized external effects than wrapper-only tool controls, while false denials are separately reported.

H4: Goodhart displacement. Evidence floors will reduce unsupported high-confidence claims but may increase evidence fabrication attempts. Confirmation requires a shift in the measured failure distribution, not merely a higher aggregate score.

8.1 Preregisterable protocol

A minimum controlled study should use multiple model families, blinded adjudication, and at least three seeds per condition. Tasks should include software environments, structured enterprise workflows, and one non-digital domain with delayed observations. Conditions are: (A) ordinary harness, (B) harness with post-hoc logging, (C) pre-commit authority and evidence gates, and (D) full prediction–outcome loop. All conditions receive matched inference, tool, and human-review budgets.

Primary endpoints are silent-drift rate, unauthorized-effect rate, false-denial rate, median correction latency, calibration error, and receipt completeness. Secondary endpoints are task success, cost, latency, escalation burden, and evidence-fabrication attempts. Promotion data and the final audit set must be isolated; a candidate is evaluated once on the private audit set after design freeze. The protocol should publish schemas, hashes, exclusions, and negative results.

9Threats to Validity

The architecture is derived from one project and reflects its terminology. Independent implementations may expose hidden coupling or unnecessary mechanisms. The telemetry snapshot is not independently verified and lacks row-level evidence in this submission. The proposed experiments have not been run. A human veto can fail through overload, capture, misunderstanding, or coercion; therefore "human in the loop" is not by itself a safety proof. Cryptographic integrity can show that a record was not changed after sealing, but cannot show that the original observation was accurate. Finally, formal policy checks prove only properties encoded in the policy and implementation, not every socially relevant value.

10Implementation and Reproducibility

A reproducible release should include: (i) machine-readable task and receipt schemas; (ii) policy fixtures for every hard gate; (iii) property-based tests proving that failed authority checks cannot reach executor primitives; (iv) an append-only prediction export with hashes; (v) a claim ledger mapping every paper statement to code, test, observation, derivation, or hypothesis; and (vi) a public threat model. Code and data links must resolve before submission.

The companion package for this manuscript includes the TeX source and two small CSV files: the disclosed aggregate snapshot and a claim-status ledger. These files document what the paper can currently support; they do not substitute for the row-level experimental artifact required by the protocol.

11Conclusion

The defensible identity of arifOS is neither quantum intelligence nor demonstrated AGI/ASI. It is a classical constitutional runtime for governing agent claims and actions, with a plausible role as an AGI/ASI governance substrate if its enforcement properties survive independent testing. Its civilizational scope remains an ambition requiring institutional legitimacy, interoperability, and deployment evidence.

Reality Engineering names the narrower engineering problem: make claims answerable to evidence, actions answerable to authority, and predictions answerable to outcomes. The scientific contribution will stand or fall not on the vocabulary, equations, or manifesto, but on preregistered comparisons, independently inspectable receipts, and the system's willingness to preserve evidence that falsifies it.

Ethics and Governance Statement

The architecture reserves irreversible or high-impact actions for explicit human authorization and treats human dignity, lawful authority, and reversibility as hard constraints. This does not transfer responsibility from deployers to software. Institutions adopting such a kernel remain responsible for lawful policy, representative governance, security, monitoring, and remedies for affected persons.

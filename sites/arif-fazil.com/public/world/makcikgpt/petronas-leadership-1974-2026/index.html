Tengku Razaleigh Hamzah

Founding Chairman & Chief Executive · 1974–1976

Appointed by: PM Tun Abdul Razak Hussein, announced 6 September 1974. Razak described the post as "a job as important as that of a Cabinet Minister… equivalent to being a Cabinet Minister".

Latar

Born 13 April 1937, Kota Bharu, Kelantan. Son of Tengku Sri Maharaja Tengku Muhammad Hamzah, a former Menteri Besar of Kelantan; great-uncle of Sultan Muhammad V. Educated at Sultan Ismail College, Malay College Kuala Kangsar and Anderson School Ipoh; BSc Economics, Queen's University Belfast, 1959. The Malaysianist records that "due to his aristocratic background, Razaleigh was wealthy" — which is why he could forgo the PETRONAS salary (exact amount never disclosed).

Before PETRONAS: founding executive director of Bank Bumiputera (1965), its chairman and managing director 1970–1976; chairman of PERNAS 1970–1974; UMNO Treasurer 1973; founded Fleet Holdings in 1972 with his own money on Tun Razak's instruction. MP for Ulu Kelantan from 1974.

Mandat — apa dia dibawa masuk untuk buat

Tun Razak's ask was not operational. As The Edge put it: Razak "chose his political protégé Tengku Razaleigh Hamzah to head the organisation as chairman, with the executive powers of a Cabinet minister." The mandate was to create a national oil company and take back the resources — the Petroleum Development Act 1974 (Royal Assent 30 July 1974, in force 1 October 1974) vests all Malaysian petroleum in PETRONAS. Razaleigh drafted it with associates in secret, before the 1974 election.

Apa dia bawa

  • Built the institutional spine. Drafted and landed the PDA 1974 — the statute that still governs PETRONAS today. Without this, nothing else in this archive exists.
  • Served notice on the majors. From 1 April 1975, any foreign company without a PETRONAS agreement was operating illegally in Malaysian waters. This triggered a 15-month standoff (Tun Raja Mohar would later mediate it).
  • Set the royalty baseline at 5% and held it against a demand from Sabah's Tun Mustapha for 10–20%.
  • First export shipment: 358,000 barrels to Japan, 1 September 1975.
  • First foreign supply deal: PETRONAS–Philippine National Oil Company, signed 11 June 1976, signed by him as Chairman and CEO.
  • First PSC architecture agreed with Shell (two) 30 November 1976 and Esso (three) 8 December 1976 — 70:30 in PETRONAS' favour. (Inference: these landed at the very end of, or just after, his tenure.)

Apa dia dapat

He gave, not took. The Malaysianist: he was "able to forgo salaries for some of the top corporate posts he held, including the Petronas job." He relinquished the PERNAS chairmanship but kept Bank Bumiputera's.

The reward was the next office: Finance Minister, 1976 to 16 July 1984 — under Hussein Onn, who had been lobbied by foreign oil companies to remove him from PETRONAS. They failed. Chairmanships of the ADB Board of Governors (1976–77), the World Bank/IMF Board of Governors (1977–78) and the Islamic Development Bank (1978–79). Honours: PSM (Tan Sri) 1973, DK Kelantan 1978, SSAP 1980, SPMS 1982.

After: MITI Minister 1984–1987; founded Bank Islam, Takaful and Bank Pembangunan; the PNB idea was his; challenged Mahathir for the UMNO presidency in 1987 and lost by 43 votes; founded Semangat 46; rejoined UMNO 1996. MP for 48 consecutive years — Ulu Kelantan 1974–1986, Gua Musang 1986–2022.

Scar / kontroversi

Fleet Holdings: paid RM32 million for 80% of Straits Times Press; by 1987 liabilities exceeded RM448 million. Michael Backman's Daim biography calls Razaleigh's Fleet finances "scandalous"; Barry Wain's reading of audited UMNO accounts says Daim inherited sound finances (malaysiakini.com/news/463024). Kelantan revoked his DK and SPMK awards in December 2010 over words uttered outside the Federal Court; DK reinstated June 2018.

Transition

Clean and unusual: he left for the Finance Ministry, and the seat passed to Tan Sri Abdul Kadir Shamsuddin. The Edge notes he "relinquished his post in 1976 when he was appointed finance minister". No succession crisis.

VERDICT — LAYAK

Reason: In twenty-six months he built the legal, fiscal and negotiating foundation of a national oil company that is now a Fortune 500 entity. The founder test is not "did he run it well" — it is "would this exist without him", and the answer is no. Two caveats, stated honestly: the first PSCs were signed at the very edge of or after his tenure, so the credit for landing them is shared with Abdul Kadir; and he was the only politician ever to hold this chair — which is precisely why every later appointment was drawn from civil servants and professionals.

Tan Sri Abdul Kadir Shamsuddin

Chairman & Chief Executive · 1976–1978

Chairman & Chief Executive · 1976 – 8 November 1978 (died in office)

Appointed by: PM Tun Abdul Razak Hussein / Tun Hussein Onn (exact date UNKNOWN). He retired from the civil service in September 1976 and took the PETRONAS seat that year.

Latar

Born 3 November 1920, Kajang, Selangor. Died 8 November 1978, aged 58, in office. Buried Jalan Ampang Muslim Cemetery. Kajang High School (1939); scholarship to Raffles College, Singapore (economics); Queen's Scholarship 1947 to read law at Bristol. (A memorial-blog book review also lists Yale; Wikipedia lists Bristol only — unresolved.)

Career: Malay Administrative Service 1948; Malayan Civil Service 1952; Assistant State Secretary Pahang; Assistant Secretary Defence 1955; Chief Assistant Secretary Defence 1957; Permanent Secretary Defence 1961; Imperial Defence College; joint secretary of the independence delegation; after 13 May 1969, Chief Administrator of the MAGERAN Public Affairs Secretariat; 4th Chief Secretary to the Government, 1 January 1970 – 30 September 1976. He converted the Federal Establishment Office into the Public Service Department. Concurrently chairman of the National Electricity Board 1973–1977.

Mandat

Inherit a two-year-old company locked in a 15-month standoff with Shell and Exxon, and convert the PDA's legal claim into actual contracts.

Apa dia bawa

  • Landed the production-sharing contract. PETRONAS' own record and his obituaries credit him with implementing the PSC — the mechanism that replaced the concession system. Shell signed two PSCs 30 November 1976 and Esso three on 8 December 1976: 70:30 to PETRONAS, 20% cost recovery (25% for gas), 10% royalty.
  • Founded Malaysia LNG Sdn Bhd, incorporated 14 June 1978 — PETRONAS 65%, with Shell Gas BV and Mitsubishi. He chaired the new company, with M.B. Hashim as managing director. This is the origin of the Bintulu LNG franchise, which first shipped cargo in January 1983.
  • He "successfully manoeuvred the company through various crises with other oil companies" (Wikipedia).
  • The Petroleum Development (Amendment) Act 1975 (Act A290) — in force 1 May 1975 — put marketing and distribution exclusively under PETRONAS, with fines to RM1 million or five years. Note: that Act predates his tenure; the standoff context is his inheritance, not his authorship.

Honest limit: the 1975 notice and the majors' non-renewal of concessions, Exxon withholding US$100m of investment, and US complaints of PETRONAS' "vacillation, lack of technical expertise" with under 40 staff — all of that is largely context he walked into, not acts he authored. Attribution of the pre-1976 events to him is inference.

Apa dia dapat

JMN 1963, PPM 1965, PSM (Tan Sri) 1966, PMN 1972, SPDK (Sabah), PNBS (Sarawak). Remuneration: UNKNOWN — no source discloses any pay figure. He also chaired the Red Crescent Society and the National Library Advisory Board.

Transition

None. He died in office on 8 November 1978. The company had to be handed to Tun Abdullah Mohd Salleh, who retired early from the Chief Secretary's post to take it on 1 January 1979. That is a genuine gap: the second chairman died with no succession plan and no deputy in place.

Scar / kontroversi

No personal scandal, investigation or misconduct allegation was found. The only critical material is era-level.

VERDICT — LAYAK

Reason: The PSC is the single mechanism that converted the PDA's paper sovereignty into real rent — and it landed on his watch, as did the founding of Malaysia LNG. His tenure is short and his death is the scar, but the two things that made PETRONAS an actual oil company rather than a regulator both date from his two years. He is the least-documented chairman of the modern era, and the record is thin enough that a hostile reading could call his personal authorship unproven. On what is sourced, the mandate was carried.

Tun Abdullah Mohd Salleh

Chairman & Chief Executive · 1979–1988

Chairman & Chief Executive · 1 January 1979 – early 1988

Appointed by: PM Tun Hussein Onn (and continued under Mahathir from 1981). He retired early from his own post to take it.

Latar

Born 24 June 1926, Kampong Batu, Padang Sebang, Malacca. Died 22 June 2006, two days short of his 80th birthday. Buried Bukit Kiara Muslim Cemetery. Malacca High School; sixth form at Malay College Kuala Kangsar; BA (Hons) Geography, University of Malaya, Singapore (Johor state scholarship).

Career: Assistant District Officer, Muar, 1955; MCS probationer 1956; Assistant State Secretary Perak; private secretary to Tunku Abdul Rahman, 1959–1962; Deputy Secretary, Public Services Commission 1962–1966; Cabinet Under Secretary, PM's Department 1967–1968; set up Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia from 1969 as its first Registrar (opened May 1970 with 192 students); Secretary-General, Ministry of Agriculture 1972–1974; Director-General of Public Services; 5th Chief Secretary to the Government, 1 October 1976 – 31 December 1978.

He then did something no other chairman did: he resigned the top civil-service post in the country to run a two-year-old company. He took the PETRONAS seat on 1 January 1979, following Abdul Kadir's death on 8 November 1978.

Mandat

Consolidate. Build domestic upstream and downstream capability on the foundation the first two chairmen had laid down.

Apa dia bawa

  • The domestic build-out years. As The Edge frames the era: "For the first 14 years, the concentration was on expanding domestic upstream and downstream activities." His nine years sit inside that window.
  • Malaysia LNG came alive under him. Incorporated June 1978, first LNG delivered January 1983 — the Bintulu franchise that became PETRONAS' cash engine.
  • Petronas Carigali (incorporated 1978) grew into the operating arm during his tenure; a 1982 Carigali–Elf Aquitaine venture established cost-recovery terms.
  • Administrative ballast. The Edge: "Other serving senior civil servants, notably Raja Mohar and Abdullah Salleh, provided the much needed administrative balance to strong characters like Rastam" — Rastam Hadi being the Deputy Governor of Bank Negara who drilled "fight like bulldogs" financial discipline into the pioneers.

Apa dia dapat

AMN 1969, DMSM 1974, PMN 1977 (Tan Sri), DPMT 1981, SSM 2003 (Tun), honorary Doctor of Laws (UKM 1980), INTAN Fellow. PETRONAS remuneration: UNKNOWN — never disclosed, in any year, for any chairman.

After: chairman of Yayasan Tun Razak (May 1989 – May 2005); non-executive director, Hongkong Bank Malaysia (1994); UKM Council chairman; president of the Family Planning Association.

Scar / kontroversi

Not personal — structural, and it is significant. The Edge, writing in 2009, describes what happened in exactly this period: "For the first 14 years, the concentration was on expanding domestic upstream and downstream activities. A comfort zone then crept in and those with a 'silo' and 'bunker' mentality emerged — the 'mine' attitude overtook the 'us' in the organisation." That critique is era-level and its attribution is inference, not a personal allegation — but the mini-empires that Azizan and Basir had to dismantle in 1988 grew during the civil-service chairmanship years. That is the honest scar of this era.

Transition

Complicated, and under-documented. He was chairman and chief executive from 1 January 1979 and is recorded as retiring as President and Chief Executive in early 1988. But by 6 November 1986 the Middle East Economic Survey was already calling Tun Raja Mohar Raja Badiozaman "Chairman of Malaysia's state oil company Petronas", and by February 1988 both men retired together — replaced by Basir Ismail (chairman) and Azizan Zainul Abidin (President/CEO). When exactly the chair was separated from Abdullah's own office is not documented. First firm evidence is 1984–86. This is the first chair/CEO separation in PETRONAS history and the record does not say who ordered it or why.

VERDICT — LAYAK (with a documented scar)

Reason: Nine years, no scandal, and the domestic industrial base — Bintulu LNG, Carigali's growth, the downstream gestation — was built on his watch. The counterweight is real: this is also the era that produced the "comfort zone", the silos and the mini-empires that the 1988 leadership had to tear down. The mandate was consolidation, and consolidation was delivered — but it consolidated into fiefdoms as well as assets. He handed over cleanly to two successors, which is more than either of his predecessors managed.

Tun Raja Mohar Raja Badiozaman

Chairman · 1980s

Chairman · UNKNOWN start (between 1979 and Nov 1986) – February 1988

GAP DECLARED: the exact start year of his PETRONAS chairmanship could not be sourced. He is documented as Chairman by MEES on 6 Nov 1986, 26 Nov 1986 and 9 Sep 1987, and as retiring in February 1988 alongside President/CEO Tun Abdullah Mohd Salleh. The handover year from Abdullah is not in any located source.

Latar

Born 23 March 1922, Kuala Kangsar, Perak. Died 8 June 2003, aged 81, of pneumonia — at the time still chairman of Perodua. Buried Ampang Muslim Cemetery. Wife: Toh Puan Norella Talib. Clifford School Kuala Kangsar; Raffles College Singapore 1946; BA (Hons) Economics, Cambridge, 1952; Diploma in Agricultural Economics, Oxford, 1953.

Career: Malay Administrative Service 1938; Malayan Civil Service 1952; Secretary-General to the Treasury, 1971–1972; Special Economic Adviser to the Prime Minister from 1972 — serving under Tun Razak, Tun Hussein Onn and Tun Mahathir, three prime ministers. He signed Malaysia's first trade deal with the USSR (1967) and helped initiate ties with China. Bernama credits him with helping establish PNB. He was one of those who knew of the PNB "dawn raid" on Guthrie on 7 September 1981; Razaleigh later said the idea arose in a golf game involving himself, Raja Mohar and Tun Ismail.

Mandat

The record does not describe an explicit mandate — and that is itself the finding. He is the archetype of the balancing chairman: a senior civil servant placed alongside a strong executive.

Apa dia bawa

  • The PSC negotiating spine. The Edge describes him as "senior civil servant, key negotiator of the country's production-sharing contract and economic adviser to the prime minister". He was also the mediator in the 15-month 1975 deadlock over the PETRONAS production-sharing agreement.
  • Administrative balance to the founding executive directors — The Edge: he and Abdullah Salleh "provided the much needed administrative balance to strong characters like Rastam".
  • The wider state apparatus. MAS chairman 1973–1991 (longest-serving), chairman of the Tourist Development Corporation, Bank Islam Malaysia, the Tun Razak Foundation, Perodua; founder-president of MAJECA 1977 and its life president from 2002; UTP Chancellor until 2004, when Mahathir succeeded him.

Honest limit: the sourced record of PETRONAS-specific decisions during his chairmanship is thin. He is a builder of the wider Malaysian state machinery who happened to hold this chair, rather than a documented transformer of PETRONAS.

Apa dia dapat

JMN 1961, PSM 1967, PMN 1977, SPCM 1985, SSSM 1992 (Tun), SSSA 1996, Japan's Order of the Sacred Treasure 1981, the Maal Hijrah award 1993. A posthumous biography, A Gracious Prince, was launched by PM Abdullah Badawi in March 2007. Remuneration as PETRONAS chairman: UNKNOWN.

Scar / kontroversi

None sourced. The search did not establish any.

Transition

Clean on paper, in the sense that he and Abdullah Salleh retired together in February 1988 and were replaced as a pair — Basir Ismail as chairman, Azizan Zainul Abidin as President/CEO. This is the moment PETRONAS' modern two-seat structure crystallised. What is not clean is that the arrangement was inherited by default: the acting-chairman vacuum of 2004–2012 shows what happens when the chair is not deliberately filled.

VERDICT — HOLDS

Reason: He held the chair competently and without scandal, and his PSC negotiation work is the most substantive thing in his file. But "hold" is the honest word: the record does not show him deciding anything distinctive at PETRONAS. He is a transition figure — the man who bridged the civil-service founding era into the professional era — and his verdict is capped by the evidentiary gap. HOLDS is not a criticism; it is a statement about the record. Where a chair leaves no documented footprint, the archive must not invent one.

Tan Sri Basir Ismail

Chairman · 1988–1995

Appointed by: PM Tun Dr Mahathir Mohamad. He had been a PETRONAS director since 1984, so this was an internal promotion to the chair.

Latar

Born 11 February 1927. Died 19 January 2007, aged 79, of a heart attack at Universiti Malaya Medical Centre. Buried at Ikhwaniah Mosque cemetery, Simpang, Taiping. Education: UNKNOWN — no source located.

Career: head of the Johor Agriculture Department; founded Johor's state economic development corporation; Bank Negara director 1978; chairman of Lembaga Padi dan Beras and the Rubber Growers Association; PETRONAS director from 1984; Bank Bumiputra executive chairman 1985–1990 (brought in to rescue it after the BMF scandal); Transport Ministry adviser; helped form Malaysia Airports Holdings Bhd and built Sepang International Circuit; Universiti Malaya council chairman 1987–1993 and board chairman 1993–2003; succeeded Mahathir as FIMA chairman in 1981; Malaysian Palm Oil Board chairman until his death. Author of Management Astray: Resuscitating Ailing Organisations.

He was, in short, Malaysia's designated corporate-rescue man — repeatedly sent into ailing institutions, PETRONAS being the one where he stayed longest.

Mandat

The 1988 reset. He arrived the same year as Azizan Zainul Abidin, and the two were handed a company The Edge describes as having developed "a comfort zone", where "those with a 'silo' and 'bunker' mentality emerged — the 'mine' attitude overtook the 'us'".

Apa dia bawa

  • He and Azizan dismantled the mini-empires. The Edge: "Together, they dismantled the 'mini-empires' in the company and emphasised the importance of teamwork and the group."
  • The unified brand. On 28 May 1989 PETRONAS launched its unified oil-drop logo under his chairmanship — consolidating subsidiaries like PETRONAS Dagangan under one identity, and shifting the colour from black to green. He and Azizan appear together in the official photograph.
  • The corporate-rescue skill set transferred inward. Financial discipline, governance, and the standing to overrule entrenched managers.
  • Sepang and Formula One. Mahathir appointed Basir, then MAHB chairman, to run the F1 negotiations; Mahathir's own tribute says he "should be remembered for… the F1 circuit". PETRONAS' F1 lineage as a Malaysian national branding project runs through this appointment.

Apa dia dapat

PSM (Tan Sri) 1984 — before he even became chairman. Chairmanships of MAHB, MPOB, PORIM, LPN, Kumpulan Fima, Universiti Malaya's board. Special Maal Hijrah award 2006. Remuneration at PETRONAS: UNKNOWN.

Scar / kontroversi

One documented optics problem: in 2001 it was reported that Basir, as MAHB chairman, offered to buy the Sepang circuit from MAHB — a conflict-of-interest question mark. The outcome of that is not sourced. No proven wrongdoing was located. Separately, his repeated role as rescuer of distressed firms (including Bank Bumiputra after BMF) means his career record includes proximity to institutional failures he was sent to clean up, not to cause.

Transition

He left as Azizan moved from President/CEO into the chair in February 1995. The Edge describes this explicitly: "Petronas' last change of guard was in February 1995, when Hassan… took over the president and CEO's post from Azizan, who assumed the position of chairman." Note what this means: the chair was handed from one insider to another insider by agreement, and the CEO seat was filled from within by Hassan Marican. 1995 is the cleanest succession in PETRONAS history.

VERDICT — LAYAK

Reason: His chairmanship is the hinge between the founding era and the professional era. The 1988 reset — dismantling fiefdoms, unifying the brand, installing Azizan's shared values — is a genuine institutional turning point, and it happened on his watch in partnership with Azizan. The attribution is divided between them, and this archive records that honestly: Azizan is credited with the DNA, Basir with the authority to enforce it. The 2001 Sepang optics is a blemish, unresolved, not a finding.

Tun Azizan Zainul Abidin

President & CEO 1988–1995 · Chairman 1995–2004

President & CEO February 1988 – February 1995 · Chairman February 1995 – 14 July 2004 (died in office)

Appointed by: PM Tun Dr Mahathir Mohamad, who recommended him for the President/CEO post in 1988. He then moved to the chair by internal transition in February 1995.

Latar

Born 28 May 1935, Air Itam, Penang. Died 14 July 2004, aged 69, at home — complained of breathing difficulties and chest pains around 4.15am. Final prayers at Putra Mosque; buried Taman Selatan Muslim cemetery, Putrajaya. Wife: Toh Puan Noor Ainee Che Teh. Children: Amir Hamzah (later EPF CEO and Minister of Finance II), Norizan, Ikhwan.

Educated at Francis Light School and Penang Free School; BA Malay Studies, University of Malaya. Joined the Ministry of Education 1960.

Career: principal private secretary to three Prime Ministers — Abdul Razak Hussein (1971–75), Hussein Onn (1975–81), Mahathir Mohamad (1981–84). Principal Assistant Secretary (security and intelligence), Home Ministry 1967; secretary of the National Security Council 1969–71; MITI Secretary-General 1984–85; Home Ministry KSU 1985–88, retiring in 1988.

Mandat

Take a company that had spent fourteen years building domestic assets inside a comfort zone, and turn it outward.

Apa dia bawa

This is the largest individual footprint in the archive.

  • Globalisation. He was "the architect of PETRONAS' globalisation plan, initially resisted by key management." First overseas exploration: Myanmar, 1990. The target was 30% of revenue from international operations by 2005; it was reached early — 30.4% of RM67.1bn revenue in 2002.
  • The shared values — PETRONAS' DNA. He institutionalised loyalty (to nation and corporation), professionalism, integrity and cohesiveness. This is the single most-quoted cultural artefact in PETRONAS' history, and it is his.
  • Dismantling the mini-empires, with Basir Ismail. The Edge records it, and former executives recall it: "Under Azizan, it was always the 'we' in the company that was emphasised… some strong individualistic attitudes were developed, where plans were considered 'my' project instead of it being 'ours'."
  • KLCC and the Twin Towers. KLCC Property's own CEO credits "the innovation and determination to build the highest building in the world by former group CEO of Petronas, the late Tun Azizan Zainul Abidin". He chaired KLCC Holdings and Suria KLCC.
  • Putrajaya. Chairman of Putrajaya Corporation — the administrative capital's development is credited to him. He is "remembered for his work in developing Putrajaya."
  • Bintulu LNG expansion — 1992 contract for three additional LNG trains, US$1.6 billion.
  • Institution-building: founded the Malaysian Petroleum Club (opened 1991 at Dayabumi). Chairmanships of Malaysia Airlines, PETRONAS Carigali, MTBE Malaysia, PETRONAS Trading Limited, Malaysia LNG, Suria KLCC. UTM Pro-Chancellor; WEF member (Malaysia hosted the Forum in 2002); chairman of the ASEAN-Canada Business Council.

Apa dia dapat

JMN 1978, PSM (Tan Sri) 1987, PNBS Sarawak 1991, DGPN Penang 2001, SSM posthumously 5 June 2010 — conferring "Tun", received by his widow. French Order of the Commander of the Legion of Honour, October 2003. Norwegian government award for outstanding contribution to quality in management, 1992. Vietnam Friendship Medal, September 2001. Named in his honour: KA Tun Azizan, a Royal Malaysian Navy auxiliary ship commissioned 5 May 2015 and deployed as an ESSCOM mobile sea base.

Remuneration: NOT FOUND. No source — in any year, for any of his offices — discloses his salary, allowances, bonus or pension.

The most quoted thing about him is his own operating principle, recalled by Shamsul Azhar Abbas: "He put himself in such a position that he could leave any time if he felt that his services were not needed, without any fear of losing anything. No agenda or self-interest to make him stay longer than necessary. So he dared to go and face his political masters. On matters of principle, if he said it could not be done, it would not be done… be it to Mahathir or anyone."

Scar / kontroversi

He died in office, still chairman of both PETRONAS and Malaysia Airlines, and a sitting commissioner of the Royal Commission on the Malaysian Police Force — he was due to discuss its findings the day he died.

And the scar is structural and enormous: his death created an eight-year governance hole. No Prime Minister appointed a successor chairman. The sitting CEO carried the chair as acting from July 2004 until June 2012. PETRONAS itself acknowledged the damage in 2012 when it said Sidek Hassan's appointment "will restore clear demarcation of roles between the chairman and president and CEO." That is the most consequential single event in the history of this seat, and it flows directly from 14 July 2004.

Transition

None — no chair successor was ever named by him or arranged with the shareholder. See above.

VERDICT — LAYAK

Reason: If one person authored modern PETRONAS, it is him. Deputy Secretary-General (Trade) 2001; Secretary-General of MITI, 24 October 2004; 12th Chief Secretary to the Government of Malaysia, 3 September 2006 – 24 June 2012 — the most senior civil servant in the country. Concurrently President of IIUM 2008–2013.

He stepped from the top of the civil service into the chair of PETRONAS within a week of retirement. That is the classic pattern — and it is exactly what the criticism in 2012 was about.

Mandat

Not stated publicly at the time. Read from what happened: restore the chair as a real, separate office. PETRONAS' own words are the mandate: his appointment "will restore clear demarcation of roles between the chairman and president and CEO, and is a further reflection of Petronas' commitment to upholding global standards of good governance."

Apa dia bawa

  • He closed the governance hole. For eight years — from Azizan's death in July 2004 — the chair had been held in an acting capacity by the sitting CEO. Hassan Marican carried it 2004–2010, Shamsul Azhar carried it 2010–2012. In that arrangement, the person running the company was also chairing the board meant to oversee him. Sidek's single most important act was simply to exist as a separate chairman. It is unglamorous and it is the most structurally consequential appointment since 1988.
  • He oversaw two CEOs. Shamsul Azhar (until 31 March 2015) and Wan Zulkiflee (from 1 April 2015).
  • A chair that stayed out of the way. There is no sourced record of him intervening in a commercial decision. The Edge's 2009 wish — "if it ain't broke, don't fix it" — was, on the record, fulfilled.
  • His tenure was extended three years in 2015, which is itself a signal: the government judged the arrangement working.
  • Chairman of the RCI into Bank Negara's 1990s forex losses — on day one, 21 August 2017, it announced a RM31.5bn loss. He drew criticism, including from Mahathir's lawyer Haniff Khatri, for reaching a conclusion on the first day.
  • Also chaired MDEC 2016–2018, and was Pro-Chancellor of UTP 2012–2018.

Apa dia dapat

PSM (Tan Sri) 2006 and PMN (Tan Sri) 2007 — both awarded while he was still Chief Secretary, before PETRONAS. Japan's Order of the Rising Sun, Gold and Silver Star (2018) — the award of a retiring senior figure.

After: Chairman of the Enforcement Agency Integrity Commission (EAIC), three-year term from 1 July 2020, replaced in February 2024 consistent with term expiry. Board member of Top Glove and Malayan Flour Mills.

Fees: no individual figure is disclosed. PETRONAS' aggregate directors' benefits were RM16m in 2021 and RM17m in 2022 for the entire board.

Scar / kontroversi

The political-appointment objection, made in his own time. Cheras UMNO chief Syed Ali Alhabshee publicly urged Najib to reconsider, arguing the post belonged to professionals, not a retired senior civil servant, and that it risked the perception that retiring officials are handed lucrative posts. That is not an outsider's complaint — it came from within the ruling party.

The structural scar — the PNW cancellation. Pacific NorthWest LNG, the Canadian bet inherited from 2012, was cancelled on 26 July 2017 during his chairmanship, with an expected ~US$800m write-off. A chairman does not run projects, and this archive does not attribute the decision to him, but the board he chaired presided over it. Record it as a board-level scar of the era.

Related, and honest: the earlier FMT reporting linking him to PETRONAS "from around 2010" is not supported. PETRONAS, The Edge and mStar all date him from 1 July 2012 — and the chair was acting-held before that. The 2010 date is unresolved in secondary reporting and is recorded here as such.

Transition

The cleanest handover in the archive. He completed his three-year contract plus a three-year extension, and left on 30 June 2018 with a named successor in place — Ahmad Nizam Salleh, effective 1 August 2018. No acting period, no gap, no interim. Six clean years. Given that his predecessor arrangement had been an eight-year vacuum, this matters more than it sounds.

VERDICT — LAYAK

Reason: He is the least commercially consequential and the most institutionally important chairman in this archive — and that is not a backhanded compliment. His mandate was governance, and governance is invisible when it works. He restored a separation of powers that had been lost for eight years, held it for six, attracted no integrity finding, took no visible operational role, and handed over cleanly to a named successor. Set beside the eight years before him, that is a substantial achievement. The scar is the Canadian cancellation that dates from his watch, and the political-perception objection his appointment attracted was real and was made by his own party. Both are recorded; neither displaces the verdict.

The Full Roster, 1974–2026

Every chairman and every president/CEO, with how each seat changed hands.

The architecture first

PETRONAS was incorporated on 17 August 1974 under the Companies Act 1965, with RM10 million paid-up capital, wholly owned by the Government of Malaysia through the Minister of Finance (Incorporated).

The seat was originally one seat. From 1974 to 1979 the office was "Chairman and Chief Executive" — a single combined post. The Petroleum Development Act 1974, s.3(2), puts PETRONAS "subject to the control and direction of the Prime Minister who may from time to time issue such direction as he may deem fit." A 1976 legal analysis of the Act is explicit: "The power of appointment of the Chairman is not reserved to a meeting of the Board of Directors… but is vested in the Prime Minister." PETRONAS Article 109, later cited in court, provides that the Prime Minister appoints the chairman, who exercises the government's proxy in accordance with the Prime Minister's directions.

The two seats separated at some point between 1979 and November 1986 (see the declared gap below) and have been separate ever since — except for eight years, 2004 to 2012, when the chair was held on an acting basis by the sitting CEO.

The founding executive directors (1974)

Two men were appointed alongside Razaleigh, and they are the reason the early company had any financial backbone at all:

  • Dato' Rastam Hadi — then Deputy Governor of Bank Negara Malaysia, assigned by Tun Razak to be an executive director. He is the man The Edge credits with the founding discipline: he "taught the pioneers to fight like bulldogs" and "drilled into the organisation the necessary management and financial discipline, turning it into one that is cautious about spending and where accountability ranks high." An employee's recorded lament from that era: "we are a very rich organisation but sometimes, it feels like being an orphan working here." Local Malaysian sources date his appointment directly by Tun Razak to the Lembaga Utama as Executive Director to 10 November 1974.
  • Dato' Wan Ismail Wan Mohd Salleh — a senior legal officer recruited from the Attorney General's Chambers. He supplied the legal and incorporation capacity, which the 1974 company needed immediately in order to contest concession rights with Shell, Esso and Mobil.

Twelve people are recorded as the founding leadership group — the chairman, the two executive directors, and the mixed group of professionals from the private sector and the civil service they recruited.

Pattern 1 — The seat began as a political hinge, then became an administrative posting

Razaleigh's chairmanship was explicitly equated to Cabinet rank, and The Malaysianist records that it was "the only time that role had executive powers". After he left to become Finance Minister, the next three chairmen came from the same small pool: the Chief Secretary's office and the Treasury. The Edge's summary is blunt — "Since then, Petronas chairmen have been selected from among senior civil servants and professionals." No politician has chaired PETRONAS since 1976.

Pattern 2 — The Chairman's seat may be filled for eight years by a CEO who was never given it

From July 2004 to July 2012, nobody was appointed chairman. The office was held in an acting capacity by whoever sat in the CEO chair. PETRONAS only described this as a defect when it ended it — the 2012 release restoring "clear demarcation of roles". Eight years, record results, and no separate board anchor above the executive.

Pattern 3 — Oil knowledge was never the qualification, and still is not

Of eleven chairmen, exactly one — Azizan — came out of PETRONAS's own operating core, and he entered as a career civil servant. The rest were a prince-politician, four senior civil servants, a corporate turnaround specialist, an accountant-CEO holding the seat by default, and three GLC generalists. Operations competence has consistently sat one tier down, in the President/CEO line. The Edge's phrase for this is the "administrative balance" the civil-service chairs provided against strong operating personalities.

Pattern 4 — The end of a CEO's term is a political event, not a performance verdict

  • Hassan Marican: fifteen years, record results, six days' notice that his contract would not be renewed.
  • Shamsul Azhar: appointed over the incumbent's own succession slate, and noted as the same age as the man he replaced.
  • Wan Zulkiflee: the groomed internal candidate, contract allowed to lapse at the change of government in 2020.
  • Tengku Muhammad Taufik: two extensions, each reported as a matter of what the Prime Minister decides.

The through-line is that the CEO seat is a renewable political appointment, and length of tenure measures political tolerance at least as much as it measures delivery.

Pattern 5 — Dividends are the real dial, and they track fiscal need as much as affordability

The declared payment moved from RM30b (FY2010) and RM26b (2015) up to RM50b (2022), RM40b (2023), RM32b (2025), then RM20b approved for 2026 — the lowest in nine years. In 2008 alone PETRONAS supplied 45% of federal revenue; the five-year figure is above 20%; the cumulative contribution since 1974 is put at nearly RM1.6 trillion. Every chairman's public record is, in part, a record of how much of that was taken out and how much was left in the ground.

What no one in the record was ever asked to justify

Pay. PETRONAS Group and its listed companies state plainly in their Corporate Governance Reports that they "opt not to disclose the personal data of their Senior Management personnel to the public at large", invoking the Personal Data Protection Act 2010. The holding company's audited financial statements disclose only an aggregate for all directors together — RM15m across the whole board in FY2025. So the individual pay of the person running a company that contributes roughly a fifth of the federal budget is not on the public record. That is the largest unresolved governance question in this archive.

The number every dossier keeps returning to

Twenty-two years. Between 1974 and 2012, some form of fused chair-and-CEO power — formally executive until about 1984, then acting-fused from 2004 to 2012 — ran for roughly twenty-two of the company's first thirty-eight years. PETRONAS's own 2012 language is the verdict on that: clear demarcation of roles is a "global standard of good governance". It took the company thirty-eight years to say so about itself.
